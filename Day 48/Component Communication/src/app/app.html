The toolbar at the bottom of this stack needs the reading list and the notifications. Nothing in between does.

With inputs and outputs, SectionA, SectionB and SectionC would each carry two inputs and two outputs they never read — twelve declarations to move two values three levels. With injection the toolbar takes nothing from its parents at all.

input() — a value the child renders
Start here. A component that is a function of its inputs can be rendered anywhere, tested without a service, and used twice on one page against different data.
output() — something happened
So the parent decides what it means. Here it means "update the list and raise a notice", which the card has no business knowing.
model() — the child owns a value
A search box's text. Not used on this page any more: list.query is asReadonly(), and a readonly signal cannot be the target of [(x)] — which is Day 47's trade showing up as a constraint.
A service — no useful common parent
The notices: raised five levels down, shown in the header, and neither is an ancestor of the other. Also the list itself, because four separate components read it.

The failure mode to watch for is the opposite of prop drilling: a service is so easy to inject that every component ends up coupled to the application, and nothing can be rendered on its own. BookCard above is the test — it still takes a book and emits events, and it still would if the service were deleted.
